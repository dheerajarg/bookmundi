## Introduction

Strategic management is not only about preparing plans; it is about understanding environmental realities, making appropriate strategic choices, and ensuring disciplined execution. This assignment presents a strategic analysis of **Paridhi Education**, a newly established education service organization in Nepal. The assignment is organized into four chapters: brief introduction of the organization, environmental scanning, strategy formulation, and strategy implementation and control. While the analysis is prepared for academic purpose, the recommendations are also designed to be practically relevant for the real growth of the organization.

## Chapter 1: Brief Introduction of the Selected Organization

Paridhi Education is a newly established education service organization based in Dhumbarahi, Kathmandu. It was established in 2082 BS (2025 AD) and registered as a profit not distributing company. Although the institution is new in legal and organizational form, it is founded by experienced education professionals who bring substantial expertise in teacher training, curriculum development, remedial education, assessment, inclusive pedagogy, digital learning, and mathematics education. Therefore, Paridhi Education should not be seen as a start-up without experience; rather, it is a new institutional platform built upon long-term professional knowledge and field engagement in Nepal's education sector.

The organization was initially conceptualized with a special concern for improving mathematics education in Nepal, an area that remains weak in many public and private schools. Over time, this concern expanded into a broader educational mission. At present, Paridhi Education aims to contribute to inclusive, equitable, and holistic quality education in Nepal through research-based and context-sensitive educational services. Its vision is that every child in Nepal should achieve his or her full potential through quality education. Its mission is to strengthen teaching, learning, and institutional practices through relevant and research-informed interventions.

Paridhi Education works in several thematic areas, including inclusive education, bridging and remedial education, early grade and mathematics education, curriculum development, assessment, digital literacy, e-learning, and educational research. It also pays attention to cross-cutting issues such as child protection, gender inclusion, climate awareness, and support for vulnerable learners. The organization provides services such as teacher professional development, training package design, lesson planning support, consultancy, digital content development, and policy or technical assistance to schools, municipalities, and education stakeholders.

One of the strongest features of Paridhi Education is the quality of its human resources. Its founders and core team members have worked in collaboration with institutions such as CEHRD, CDC, NGOs, and local education programs. Their experience in mathematics, remedial learning, and local language-based training in Nepali, Maithili, and Bajika gives the organization a valuable contextual advantage, especially in provinces such as Madhesh. In the Nepali context, where many education interventions fail because they are technically sound but locally weak, this combination of technical depth and local familiarity is a major institutional strength.

From a strategic point of view, Paridhi Education can be positioned as a mission-driven, professional, and specialized education organization. Its future growth will depend on how effectively it converts its technical competence into institutional credibility, sustainable revenue streams, and long-term partnerships.

## Chapter 2: Environmental Scanning

Environmental scanning is a vital step for Paridhi Education because the organization is entering a complex and changing education market in Nepal. At present, success in the education sector depends not only on technical expertise but also on political awareness, donor trends, relationship management, institutional trust, and the ability to survive in an uncertain funding environment. In this chapter, the external environment is analyzed through **PESTEL** and **Porter's Five Forces**, while the internal environment is examined through **VRIO** and **SWOT**. Additional emphasis is given to current realities in Nepal, including political instability, shrinking donor support, and the difficulty that smaller organizations face in securing work.

### 2.1 External Environment Analysis through PESTEL

**Political and Legal Environment**

The political environment of Nepal remains one of the most significant external factors affecting organizations like Paridhi Education. Nepal has experienced frequent government changes, coalition reshuffles, and policy uncertainty for many years. Reuters has noted that since the abolition of monarchy in 2008, Nepal has seen repeated government instability, and even in recent years coalition changes have directly affected governance continuity. Such instability does not only affect national politics; it also influences project approval, budget execution, procurement timelines, and policy implementation at the federal, provincial, and local levels.

For organizations working in education, this situation creates both risk and complexity. In formal terms, public and donor-supported assignments are expected to follow transparent procedures. However, in practice, access to information, trust-building, and project opportunities are often influenced by informal networks, reputation, and in some cases political connection. Many small and technically strong organizations struggle not because they lack capacity, but because they do not have sufficient access to decision-makers, procurement channels, or influential institutional networks. In the Nepali context, therefore, political neutrality alone is not enough; organizations also need strong stakeholder engagement, institutional visibility, and consistent relationship management.

There is another political dimension as well. The recent political upheaval, youth protests, anti-corruption sentiment, and shifting power centers indicate that the governance environment is becoming more sensitive and unpredictable. For Paridhi Education, this means that depending excessively on politically influenced assignments would be risky. The organization should instead pursue an ethical and non-partisan relationship strategy that allows it to work with different stakeholders without being identified too closely with any one political group.

**Economic Environment**

The economic context of Nepal is mixed and somewhat difficult for new service organizations. Inflationary pressure, constrained public expenditure, delayed transfers to provincial and local governments, and weak budget execution reduce the spending capacity of institutions. Reports in 2025 highlighted that incomplete fiscal transfers and systemic delays were affecting implementation at provincial and local levels. For Paridhi Education, this means that even when there is demand for educational support, contracts may be delayed, payments may not be timely, and approved programs may not start according to plan.

The economic challenge has become more serious because of shrinking donor support. A major example is the suspension and termination of several USAID-funded programs in Nepal after the U.S. aid freeze in 2025. Reporting by Kathmandu Post indicated that projects worth billions of rupees were affected, including projects in education and other development sectors. When such a major donor withdraws or reduces support, the effect is not limited to large INGOs alone. It creates a chain reaction across the whole ecosystem: fewer tenders, stronger competition for remaining grants, lower subcontracting opportunities, and growing pressure on local organizations. For small organizations like Paridhi Education, the direct implication is clear: depending heavily on donor-funded consulting alone is increasingly risky.

However, the economic environment is not entirely negative. There is still increasing demand for high-impact and cost-effective educational services, especially where schools, municipalities, and development actors are seeking measurable improvement in learning outcomes. In such a situation, organizations that can offer practical, specialized, and outcome-oriented support can still build a market.

**Social Environment**

The social environment is strongly favorable to Paridhi Education. Across Nepal, concern is rising over weak foundational learning, poor mathematics achievement, school dropout, remedial learning needs, and unequal access to quality education. Parents, schools, municipalities, and development organizations are more aware than before that many children are attending school without achieving expected competencies. The learning loss created by past disruptions, along with continued inequality among regions and social groups, has made remedial and inclusive education more important than ever.

This directly supports Paridhi Education's mission. Its focus on mathematics, bridging classes, teacher development, inclusive pedagogy, and local-language-sensitive training is closely aligned with actual social needs in Nepal. For example, in districts where early grade mathematics remains weak, the organization can provide targeted teacher coaching, diagnostic assessment, and remedial lesson planning. Likewise, in municipalities trying to re-engage vulnerable children, Paridhi can support bridging education and inclusive teacher capacity-building.

**Technological Environment**

Technology is gradually reshaping Nepal's education sector. Digital communication, online meetings, teacher support groups, learning videos, simple assessment tools, and hybrid training models are becoming more common. Although digital inequality remains a concern, schools and teachers are more open to technology-supported learning than in the past. This creates an opportunity for Paridhi Education to develop blended models of service delivery. Instead of depending only on face-to-face training, the organization can provide digital follow-up materials, WhatsApp or LMS-based support, video demonstrations, and electronic training resources. This can reduce delivery cost and increase post-training engagement.

**Environmental Context**

Nepal's exposure to floods, landslides, earthquakes, and climate-related disruptions also affects school continuity and program delivery. Educational organizations that understand this reality and design flexible learning and training models will be more resilient. Paridhi Education's broader concern for cross-cutting issues like climate and child vulnerability strengthens its relevance in this area.

### 2.2 Industry Analysis through Porter's Five Forces

Porter's Five Forces suggests that the education consultancy and training market in Nepal is moderately to highly competitive.

**Threat of new entrants:** Entry barriers are not very high because new firms can be established with limited physical investment. However, real entry into the market requires trust, past performance, and professional credibility. This means new organizations can enter easily on paper, but not easily in practice.

**Bargaining power of buyers:** Buyers such as municipalities, schools, NGOs, and donor-funded projects have high bargaining power. They compare providers on cost, prior experience, reporting capacity, and relationship strength. For a new organization, this puts pressure on pricing and proposal quality.

**Bargaining power of suppliers:** In Paridhi Education's case, suppliers mainly refer to expert trainers, researchers, assessors, content developers, and field coordinators. Since specialized expertise in inclusive pedagogy and early grade mathematics is limited, quality human resources have moderate bargaining power.

**Threat of substitutes:** Internal trainers, free online materials, universities, freelance consultants, and NGO technical teams can function as substitutes. Therefore, Paridhi Education must provide contextualized and implementation-oriented solutions rather than generic training packages.

**Competitive rivalry:** Rivalry is strong. Established NGOs, consulting firms, independent experts, and politically or institutionally connected organizations compete for similar assignments. After donor contraction, the rivalry is likely to become even more intense.

### 2.3 Internal Environment Analysis through VRIO and SWOT

The **VRIO framework** shows that Paridhi Education possesses several strategically important resources. Its expertise in mathematics education, remedial learning, inclusive pedagogy, teacher training, and local language delivery is clearly **valuable** because these are urgent needs in Nepal. This combination is relatively **rare**, especially when combined with local contextual knowledge. It is moderately difficult to **imitate** because it is based on long field experience, professional networks, and practical learning rather than only academic qualification. The main weakness is the **organization** dimension: since Paridhi is new, it still needs stronger systems for branding, documentation, proposal management, finance, and scaling.

The internal condition can also be summarized through **SWOT**:

**Strengths:** experienced technical team; strong expertise in mathematics and inclusive education; local language advantage; practical field experience; research-based orientation; flexible and mission-driven identity.

**Weaknesses:** new brand identity; limited organizational capital; limited institutional visibility; dependence on founders for both technical and business development roles; possible cash-flow constraints during delayed payments.

**Opportunities:** rising concern over learning outcomes; increasing need for remedial and bridging education; digital and hybrid learning demand; space for locally grounded organizations after donor localization trends; municipality-level education strengthening initiatives.

**Threats:** shrinking donor support; heavy competition for assignments; project access shaped by informal networks; procurement delays; payment delays; political instability; and the risk of being underpriced by competitors.

### 2.4 Present Strategic Difficulties Faced by Organizations like Paridhi Education

At the present time, organizations like Paridhi Education face a very real survival challenge in Nepal. First, they often struggle to win initial projects because they lack institutional track record even when they have excellent professionals. Second, in many cases, technically strong local organizations remain outside opportunity channels because project information travels through networks before it reaches open competition. Third, donor contraction has increased competition and reduced the number of available assignments. Fourth, new organizations face compliance burdens such as registration, tax, documentation, proposal writing, audit readiness, and reporting standards, all of which require time and money. Finally, many founder-led organizations become overstretched because the same people handle networking, technical delivery, administration, and financial management.

Despite these challenges, Paridhi Education also has a genuine opportunity. The present environment is making local credibility, contextual knowledge, cost-effectiveness, and measurable impact more important than before. If the organization can convert these into a clear strategic position, it can grow gradually and sustainably.

## Chapter 3: Strategy Formulation

Based on the above analysis, the most appropriate strategic choice for Paridhi Education is a **focused differentiation strategy supported by selective partnership and revenue diversification**. The core logic is that Paridhi Education should not try to become a broad-based education consultancy immediately. Instead, it should deliberately position itself as a **specialist organization in mathematics improvement, remedial and bridging education, and inclusive teacher development**.

This strategy is justified in several ways. First, from the viewpoint of **vision and mission alignment**, it fits the organization's commitment to quality, inclusive, and research-based education. Second, from a **resource-based view**, the strategy builds on Paridhi's strongest internal resources: mathematics expertise, inclusive pedagogy, local language capability, and experience in teacher development. Third, from the perspective of **Porter's generic strategy**, differentiation is more realistic than price competition because the organization cannot sustainably underbid larger or politically connected competitors. Fourth, using **SWOT logic**, this strategy matches internal strengths with external opportunities while reducing the impact of weaknesses such as limited capital and new market presence. Finally, using the **SAF approach**, the strategy is suitable to current market needs, acceptable to clients seeking quality and accountability, and feasible for a small but technically competent organization.

However, focused differentiation alone is not enough in the current Nepali environment. Therefore, it should be supported by two additional strategic directions.

The first is a **collaborative strategy**. Paridhi Education should form partnerships or consortium arrangements with established NGOs, consulting firms, municipalities, universities, and school networks. This will help the organization enter larger assignments without carrying the full burden of business development alone. For example, in a donor-funded education project, Paridhi can join as a specialist partner on mathematics, remedial learning, assessment, or inclusive pedagogy.

The second is **revenue diversification**. Due to shrinking donor funds, the organization should not rely only on consultancy contracts. It should develop three revenue streams: institutional training and consulting; school-based service packages; and paid academic products such as diagnostic tools, teacher manuals, digital learning content, or mathematics support packages. In this way, the organization can reduce vulnerability to donor fluctuation.

Therefore, the recommended strategic position for Paridhi Education is this: **be known as a trusted local specialist in mathematics and inclusive learning, grow through partnerships, and reduce risk through diversified but related revenue streams.**

## Chapter 4: Strategy Implementation and Control

To translate the above strategy into practice, Paridhi Education should implement a one-year strategic program titled **"Math and Inclusive Learning Excellence Initiative."** The purpose of this program should be to build institutional credibility while also generating sustainable business. The program may begin in selected municipalities, school networks, or development projects where mathematics improvement, teacher support, and remedial learning are immediate needs.

### 4.1 Program and Tactics

The implementation should include the following practical tactics:

1. **Develop flagship service packages.**  
   Paridhi Education should design 3-4 standard packages such as:  
   - Foundational Mathematics Improvement Package  
   - Bridging and Remedial Learning Support Package  
   - Inclusive Classroom and Teacher Development Package  
   - Diagnostic Assessment and School Improvement Support Package

2. **Target clients strategically.**  
   Rather than approaching everyone, the organization should target three segments first:  
   - municipalities and local governments,  
   - donor or NGO projects needing technical partners, and  
   - private or community school networks needing subject-specific improvement.

3. **Build ethical relationship capital.**  
   Since project access in Nepal often depends on networks, Paridhi Education should invest in non-partisan and professional relationship-building. This includes regular meetings with municipalities, participation in education forums, maintaining contact with NGOs and education officers, presenting evidence of past work, and building visibility through publications and professional events. This is an ethical alternative to dependence on political patronage.

4. **Strengthen credibility through evidence.**  
   Every assignment should produce visible outputs and measurable results. If a municipality engages Paridhi for mathematics support, the organization should document pre-training and post-training data, teacher reflections, classroom change, and student learning progress. Evidence will gradually become the organization's strongest marketing tool.

5. **Reduce financial vulnerability.**  
   The organization should negotiate advance payments where possible, maintain a small operational reserve, and avoid overdependence on a single donor or client. A practical rule may be that no single client should account for more than 40 percent of annual revenue.

An indicative annual budget for this strategy may be structured as follows:

### 4.3 Procedures for Implementation

Implementation should follow a clear operational cycle: opportunity identification, client consultation, need assessment, proposal development, agreement finalization, program design, delivery, follow-up support, documentation, and reporting. Each assignment should have a designated focal person, approved timeline, client communication record, budget sheet, and reporting template. Standard operating procedures should also be developed for training preparation, financial approval, travel management, invoice submission, and post-program review.

### 4.4 Performance Monitoring and Control

Performance monitoring should include both operational and strategic indicators. Suggested indicators include:

- number of proposals submitted,
- proposal success rate,
- number of institutional clients secured,
- number of teachers trained,
- participant satisfaction level,
- change in teacher competency,
- evidence of student mathematics improvement,
- percentage of repeat clients,
- average payment collection time,
- revenue share by client type,
- budget variance, and
- staff or associate utilization rate.

Monthly operational review and quarterly strategic review should be mandatory. If expected performance is not achieved, corrective action should be taken immediately. For example, if proposals are not successful, the organization should revise proposal quality, partnership strategy, or targeting approach. If cash flow becomes weak, it should reduce non-essential expenses and increase smaller direct-service products. If donor-funded work declines further, the organization should accelerate market development toward municipalities, school groups, and fee-based academic services.

### 4.5 Strategic Recommendations for Real Organizational Benefit

For genuine long-term benefit, Paridhi Education should consider the following strategic actions:

- build a visible brand around mathematics and inclusive learning rather than a broad generic education identity;
- prepare 2-3 high-quality concept notes and capability statements that can be shared quickly with potential partners;
- create a database of municipalities, NGOs, schools, and education officers for structured follow-up;
- publish short evidence notes, blog articles, or learning briefs to strengthen thought leadership;
- use small pilot projects to generate testimonials and references;
- maintain political neutrality but invest strongly in institutional relationship-building;
- join or collaborate with larger consortia instead of competing alone in every large bid;
- develop at least one steady non-donor revenue source within 12 months; and
- gradually institutionalize systems so that the organization does not depend only on its founders.

In summary, strategy implementation for Paridhi Education should not only aim at delivering projects. It should also aim at building legitimacy, visibility, resilience, and financial sustainability in an uncertain Nepali environment.

## Conclusion

This assignment shows that Paridhi Education has strong potential to become a meaningful education organization in Nepal, especially in the fields of mathematics improvement, remedial education, and inclusive teacher support. The external environment is challenging due to political instability, shrinking donor funding, procurement delays, and intense competition. At the same time, the social need for quality education support remains very high. The internal analysis shows that Paridhi Education possesses valuable technical strengths, but it must strengthen its organizational systems, revenue model, and market access strategy.

The most suitable path forward is a focused differentiation strategy supported by partnership-building and revenue diversification. If implemented with discipline, evidence, and ethical stakeholder engagement, this strategy can help Paridhi Education grow not only as an academic case for this assignment but also as a practical and sustainable institution in Nepal's education sector.
