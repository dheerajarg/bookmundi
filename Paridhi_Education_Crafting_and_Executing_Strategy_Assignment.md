# Crafting and Executing Strategy: Paridhi Education

## Chapter 1: Brief Introduction of Selected Organization

Paridhi Education is a newly established education service organization based in Dhumbarahi, Kathmandu, registered in 2082 BS (2025 AD) as a profit not distributing company. Although the organization is new in legal form, it is founded by experienced education professionals who have already worked in teacher training, curriculum development, inclusive education, remedial learning, assessment, digital literacy, and especially mathematics education in Nepal. This gives Paridhi Education a strong professional base despite being at an early organizational stage.

The organization was initially conceptualized to address one of the most neglected but critical areas of Nepali schooling: mathematics learning. Over time, its vision expanded beyond mathematics alone into a broader commitment to quality education, including inclusive education, bridging and remedial education, curriculum support, digital learning, and educational research. Its stated vision is that every child in Nepal should achieve full potential through inclusive, equitable, and holistic education. Its mission is to provide research-based educational services that strengthen teaching, learning, and systemic practices across Nepal.

Paridhi Education offers services such as teacher professional development, lesson plan and material development, research and consultancy, bridging class design, remedial education support, digital education solutions, and policy support for schools and local governments. The organization also has thematic strength in early grade learning, foundational numeracy, disability-inclusive teaching, and context-sensitive training in Nepali, Maithili, and Bajika.

In the Nepali context, Paridhi Education can be positioned as a specialized education enterprise with strong social purpose. Its greatest potential lies in combining technical expertise, local relevance, and innovation to improve learning outcomes, particularly in mathematics and inclusive education, where many schools still face serious quality gaps.

## Chapter 2: Environmental Scanning

Environmental scanning is essential for Paridhi Education because it is a newly established organization working in Nepal's changing education sector. For this analysis, external factors are examined through PESTEL and Porter's Five Forces, while internal factors are assessed through VRIO and SWOT.

### 2.1 External Environment Analysis

Using **PESTEL analysis**, the first issue is the **political-legal environment**. Education in Nepal is shaped by government policy, local government priorities, donor-supported projects, curriculum reforms, and procurement procedures. This creates opportunity because municipalities and development partners increasingly invest in teacher training, inclusive education, and learning recovery. However, policy inconsistency, bureaucratic delay, and compliance requirements can slow project execution for a new organization.

The **economic environment** is mixed. On one side, inflation and budget pressure affect school spending and limit the ability of institutions to pay for consultancy services. On the other side, demand for cost-effective, high-impact education support is increasing, especially where poor learning outcomes are visible. This creates room for organizations that can show measurable results rather than only deliver general training.

The **social environment** is highly favorable for Paridhi Education. In Nepal, concern is growing over weak foundational learning, mathematics anxiety, school dropout, and inequality in access to quality education. There is also rising awareness regarding disability inclusion, gender sensitivity, and remedial support for lagging learners. Since Paridhi Education works with child-centered and inclusive principles, its service direction fits well with current social needs.

The **technological environment** is also promising. Schools, teachers, and training providers are increasingly using online platforms, digital content, and technology-supported communication. Though digital access remains unequal, there is clear opportunity for Paridhi Education to combine physical training with digital resources, follow-up support, and simple assessment tools. The **environmental context** of Nepal, including disaster risk and school disruption, further increases the need for flexible and resilient learning support models.

### 2.2 Industry/Operating Environment

Porter's **Five Forces** shows a moderately competitive sector. The **threat of new entrants** is medium because starting an education consultancy does not require heavy capital, but building credibility and professional trust takes time. The **bargaining power of buyers** is high because schools, municipalities, NGOs, and donor projects can compare several providers and usually expect both quality and low cost. The **bargaining power of suppliers** is moderate because quality trainers and education experts are limited. The **threat of substitutes** is also present through free online materials, in-house trainers, and NGO resource persons. **Competitive rivalry** is strong, especially for donor-funded and training-based assignments. Therefore, Paridhi Education cannot succeed by competing on price alone; it must compete on specialization and impact.

### 2.3 Internal Environment Analysis

Using the **VRIO framework**, Paridhi Education has strong potential. Its expertise in early grade mathematics, teacher training, remedial learning, inclusive pedagogy, and local language delivery is clearly **valuable** in Nepal's present education context. This mix of technical and contextual capability is relatively **rare**. It is also not easy to **imitate** because it comes from long field experience and professional engagement with institutions such as CEHRD, CDC, and NGOs. The main weakness lies in **organization**: as a new firm, Paridhi Education still needs stronger systems for branding, documentation, scaling, and financial management.

From a practical internal perspective, its strengths lie in program design, training delivery, educational content development, and stakeholder engagement. Its weaknesses are limited brand recognition, limited capital, and dependence on a small core team. These points can be summarized through **SWOT** as follows: strengths are technical expertise, credibility, multilingual reach, and inclusive orientation; weaknesses are newness, small scale, and system limitations; opportunities are rising demand for mathematics improvement, teacher development, digital learning, and bridging education; threats are procurement delay, price pressure, and competition from established organizations.

Overall, the environmental scan suggests that Paridhi Education has good market potential, but its success depends on focused positioning, quality delivery, and gradual expansion rather than broad competition from the beginning.

## Chapter 3: Strategy Formulation

Based on the above analysis, Paridhi Education should adopt a **focused differentiation strategy** in the first three years of operation. This means the organization should not try to serve all segments of the education market equally. Instead, it should deliberately focus on a high-need niche where it already has competence and credibility: **mathematics improvement, remedial/bridging education, and inclusive teacher development for schools, municipalities, and development partners in Nepal**.

This strategy is suitable because the environmental scan shows two realities. First, Nepal has a growing demand for quality improvement in foundational learning, especially mathematics and inclusive education. Second, Paridhi Education does not yet have the scale or capital to compete broadly with large NGOs or established consultancies. A focused differentiation strategy allows the organization to build a clear identity around specialized, research-based, and context-sensitive educational solutions.

From the internal perspective, this strategy aligns with Paridhi Education's strongest competencies: expert trainers, knowledge of early grade mathematics, inclusive pedagogy, remedial learning, local language support, and experience in training design. Using SWOT logic, the strategy matches strengths with opportunities while reducing the weakness of limited resources. From the SAF perspective, it is suitable to market conditions, acceptable to stakeholders who value quality and impact, and feasible for a new organization with limited capital but high technical competence.

In practical terms, Paridhi Education should position itself as a **specialist partner for measurable learning improvement**, not merely as a general training provider. Its flagship offer can be a "Mathematics and Inclusive Learning Support Package" including teacher training, diagnostic assessment, remedial lesson plans, classroom follow-up, and digital support materials. This strategic focus will help the organization build reputation, secure repeat contracts, and later expand into broader educational services. In the Nepali context, such a disciplined niche strategy is more realistic and sustainable than premature diversification.

## Chapter 4: Strategy Implementation and Control

To implement the focused differentiation strategy, Paridhi Education should begin with a one-year flagship program called **Math and Inclusive Learning Excellence Initiative**. The program should target 2-3 pilot municipalities or school networks and offer mathematics teacher training, diagnostic assessment, remedial lesson planning, classroom follow-up, and digital support materials. The main tactics are client selection, customized delivery, follow-up coaching, and documentation of results.

### 4.2 Procedures and Control

Implementation should follow a clear procedure: need assessment, proposal approval, program design, delivery, follow-up coaching, and final reporting. Each project should have a focal person, timeline, and budget control sheet, with standard forms for attendance, assessment, feedback, and expense tracking.

Performance should be monitored through indicators such as number of clients secured, teachers trained, satisfaction level, change in teacher competency, student mathematics improvement, repeat contracts, and budget variance. Monthly internal reviews and quarterly strategic reviews should compare target and actual performance. If results fall below expectation, the organization should revise content, trainer deployment, or client targeting. This will keep implementation measurable and aligned with organizational vision.
