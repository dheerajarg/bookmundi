1.0 Introduction

In the present competitive and uncertain environment, no organization can survive only on good intention, technical skill, or hard work. Every organization, whether large or small, profit-oriented or socially driven, needs a clear strategic direction. This is especially true in Nepal, where institutions operate in a context shaped by political instability, changing public priorities, uneven market opportunities, shrinking donor support, and growing demand for accountability. In such a setting, strategy is not just a management tool; it becomes a necessity for survival, growth, and long-term relevance.

From my understanding, the concept of crafting and executing strategy is particularly important for newly established organizations. Many organizations begin with a strong purpose and a capable team, but they struggle when they face real-world constraints such as limited resources, weak institutional visibility, delayed payments, bureaucratic hurdles, and difficulty in accessing projects. This is where strategic management becomes meaningful. It helps an organization understand where it stands, what opportunities and threats exist around it, what strengths it can build upon, and what practical course of action it should take. In other words, strategy connects vision with action.

For this assignment, I have selected Paridhi Education as the organization of study. I have chosen this organization not only because it is newly established, but also because it represents an important and realistic case in the Nepali context. Paridhi Education has been formed with the aim of improving quality education in Nepal, with major focus on mathematics, teacher support, inclusive education, and remedial learning. In my view, this makes it a highly suitable organization for strategic analysis because it operates in a sector where social need is very high, but operational and financial sustainability is often difficult to achieve.

Another reason why this organization is meaningful for analysis is that it reflects the present reality of many professional local institutions in Nepal. Such organizations may have strong human resources, technical experience, and field-based understanding, yet they still face difficulty in getting work, building market credibility, and competing with larger or better-connected institutions. Therefore, this assignment is not only an academic exercise. It is also an attempt to examine how a mission-driven Nepali organization can position itself strategically and grow in a difficult environment.

This report is divided into four major chapters. The first chapter presents a brief introduction of Paridhi Education. The second chapter examines the internal and external environment of the organization using strategic management tools such as PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, VRIO, and SWOT. The third chapter proposes an appropriate strategy for the organization with logical justification. The fourth chapter discusses the practical side of implementation and control, including programs, tactics, budget, procedures, and performance monitoring mechanisms.

Overall, this assignment attempts to combine theory and practice. While the structure follows the academic requirements of the MBA course, the analysis has also been developed in a way that can offer practical value for the actual growth of Paridhi Education. In my opinion, that is what makes strategic management truly meaningful: it should not remain limited to classroom understanding, but should help organizations make better decisions in real life.

2.0 Chapter One: Brief Introduction of the Selected Organization

2.1 Organizational Background

Paridhi Education is a newly established education service organization based in Dhumbarahi, Kathmandu. It was established in 2082 BS (2025 AD) and registered as a profit not distributing company. Although the organization is new in legal and institutional form, it is founded by experienced education professionals who already possess significant expertise in teacher training, curriculum development, remedial learning, educational assessment, inclusive pedagogy, digital learning, and mathematics education. For this reason, Paridhi Education should not be understood merely as a start-up with limited experience; rather, it is a new institutional platform built on prior professional knowledge and practice in Nepal’s education sector.

2.2 Origin and Purpose

The organization was initially conceptualized with a special concern for improving mathematics education in Nepal. This concern is highly relevant because mathematics remains one of the most challenging subject areas for many students in both public and private schools. Over time, however, the scope of the organization expanded from mathematics alone to a broader commitment toward quality education, inclusive learning, teacher development, and evidence-based educational support.

The core purpose of Paridhi Education is to strengthen teaching, learning, and educational systems in Nepal through research-based, context-sensitive, and practically relevant educational services. In my view, this is a strong and appropriate purpose because Nepal’s education system does not only need more programs; it needs more meaningful and effective interventions that respond to real classroom problems.

2.3 Vision, Mission, and Areas of Work

Paridhi Education envisions a Nepal where every child can achieve his or her full potential through inclusive, equitable, and holistic education. Its mission is to provide research-based educational services that strengthen teaching, learning, and institutional practices across Nepal. This mission reflects both social commitment and professional seriousness.

The organization works in several thematic areas, including inclusive education, bridging and remedial education, early grade and mathematics education, curriculum development, assessment, digital literacy, e-learning, and educational research. In addition, it pays attention to important cross-cutting concerns such as child protection, gender inclusion, climate awareness, and support for vulnerable learners.

Its service portfolio includes teacher professional development, training package design, lesson planning support, development of contextualized teaching-learning materials, consultancy, digital content development, and policy or technical support to schools, municipalities, and other education stakeholders.

2.4 Human Resource Strength and Strategic Significance

One of the major strengths of Paridhi Education is its team. Its founders and core members have worked with institutions such as CEHRD, CDC, NGOs, and local education programs. Their experience in mathematics, remedial learning, inclusive pedagogy, and local language-based training in Nepali, Maithili, and Bajika creates a valuable contextual advantage. This is especially important in provinces such as Madhesh, where language, trust, and local understanding directly affect the quality of educational work.

In the Nepali context, many interventions fail not because the technical idea is weak, but because the approach does not fit the local reality. Therefore, I believe the combination of technical depth and local familiarity makes Paridhi Education strategically important and potentially valuable in Nepal’s education landscape.

2.5 Overall Strategic Position

From a strategic point of view, Paridhi Education can be positioned as a mission-driven, professionally oriented, and specialized education organization. Its long-term growth will depend on how effectively it converts its technical competence into institutional credibility, sustainable revenue, and long-term partnerships. As a newly established organization, the challenge before Paridhi Education is not only to do good work, but also to become visible, trusted, and financially sustainable.

3.0 Chapter Two: Environmental Scanning

3.1 Meaning and Relevance of Environmental Scanning

Environmental scanning is a vital process for Paridhi Education because the organization is entering a complex and changing education market in Nepal. In the current context, success in the education sector depends not only on technical expertise but also on political awareness, donor trends, relationship management, institutional trust, and the ability to survive in an uncertain funding environment. Since Paridhi Education is still in its early phase, it is especially important for the organization to understand the environment before making long-term strategic commitments.

In this chapter, the external environment is analyzed through PESTEL and Porter’s Five Forces, while the internal environment is examined through VRIO and SWOT. Special emphasis is also given to current realities in Nepal, including political instability, shrinking donor support, bureaucratic complexity, and the practical difficulty that smaller organizations face in securing projects.

3.2 External Environment Analysis through PESTEL

3.2.1 Political and Legal Environment

The political environment of Nepal remains one of the most influential external factors for organizations like Paridhi Education. Nepal has experienced repeated government changes, coalition reshuffles, unstable alliances, and policy uncertainty for many years. This instability affects not only national political leadership but also governance continuity, budget release, project approval, public procurement, and local implementation systems. In practice, when government leadership changes or political priorities shift, many development processes slow down or become uncertain.

For an education service organization, this situation creates both strategic risk and operational complexity. Formally, public and donor-supported assignments are expected to follow transparent procedures. However, the reality in Nepal is often more complicated. In many cases, access to information, trust-building, and project opportunities are influenced by informal networks, institutional familiarity, and sometimes political connection. This is a sensitive issue, but it is also a practical reality. Many technically strong local organizations struggle to get work not because they lack capacity, but because they do not have enough access to decision-makers, procurement channels, or influential networks.

In my opinion, this is one of the biggest practical challenges for a new organization like Paridhi Education. It means that professional competence alone may not be enough in the initial stage. The organization also needs to build visibility, credibility, and relationships in an ethical and non-partisan way. It should avoid dependence on political patronage, but it cannot afford to remain invisible in a network-based operating environment.

Recent developments in Nepal also suggest a more sensitive political atmosphere, with growing public frustration around corruption, governance failure, and exclusionary decision-making. This creates a mixed environment. On one hand, instability creates uncertainty. On the other hand, it increases demand for transparent, accountable, and locally trusted institutions. If Paridhi Education can maintain professional neutrality and build trust across multiple stakeholder groups, this can become a strategic advantage.

3.2.2 Economic Environment

The economic context of Nepal is difficult for newly established service organizations. Inflation, slow economic activity, budget pressure, delayed fiscal transfers, and weak expenditure capacity at provincial and local levels reduce the spending ability of institutions. In practical terms, this means that even when there is real demand for educational support, contracts may be delayed, approved budgets may not be released on time, and payment cycles may become uncertain.

Another major issue is shrinking donor support. A significant example is the suspension and cancellation of several USAID-funded programs in Nepal following the U.S. aid freeze in 2025. This development has affected the broader aid ecosystem in Nepal. When large donor-supported programs contract, the impact spreads downward to implementing partners, consultants, training providers, local NGOs, and specialist organizations. For small institutions such as Paridhi Education, the implication is serious: there are fewer opportunities, more competition, and stronger pricing pressure.

This situation also teaches an important strategic lesson. Organizations like Paridhi Education cannot rely only on donor-funded assignments for sustainability. They need to think beyond project dependency and build a mixed revenue model. At the same time, the economic environment still offers opportunity because schools, municipalities, and development partners continue to demand cost-effective and high-impact educational support. Therefore, organizations that can demonstrate clear value and measurable outcomes can still survive and grow.

3.2.3 Social Environment

The social environment is highly favorable for Paridhi Education. Across Nepal, concern is increasing over weak foundational learning, poor mathematics performance, school dropout, remedial learning needs, and unequal access to quality education. It is now widely recognized that many students are attending school but not learning at the expected level. This concern is even stronger in foundational subjects such as mathematics, where many students face fear, low confidence, and weak conceptual understanding.

This social context fits strongly with the mission of Paridhi Education. Its focus on mathematics, bridging classes, teacher development, inclusive pedagogy, and local-language-sensitive training is directly related to the real needs of schools and communities. For example, if a municipality is concerned about poor foundational numeracy in its schools, Paridhi Education can provide mathematics-focused teacher training, diagnostic assessment, and remedial planning. Likewise, if there are out-of-school or learning-lagged children, the organization can support bridging education in a more structured way.

3.2.4 Technological Environment

Technology is gradually changing how education support is delivered in Nepal. Online meetings, digital training content, virtual mentoring, mobile-based communication, and blended learning models are becoming more acceptable. Although digital inequality still exists, teachers and institutions are more open than before to technology-supported approaches. This creates a strategic opportunity for Paridhi Education to combine face-to-face work with digital follow-up, electronic materials, teacher support groups, and simple online assessment tools. Such a blended model can help the organization reduce cost, improve consistency, and stay connected with clients after formal training is completed.

3.2.5 Environmental Context

Nepal is highly vulnerable to floods, landslides, earthquakes, and other disruptions that affect school continuity and field operations. Educational organizations that understand these realities and design flexible support systems will be more resilient. Since Paridhi Education already pays attention to climate and child vulnerability as cross-cutting issues, it has the potential to create educational solutions that are both academically useful and contextually relevant.

3.3 Industry Analysis through Porter’s Five Forces

Porter’s Five Forces analysis suggests that the education consultancy and training market in Nepal is moderately to highly competitive.

Threat of New Entrants:
Entry barriers are not very high because a new consultancy can be registered with limited physical investment. However, actual market entry is much more difficult because credibility, references, networks, and trust take time to build. Therefore, while many organizations can enter in formal terms, only some can build a sustainable position.

Bargaining Power of Buyers:
Buyers such as municipalities, schools, NGOs, and donor-funded projects have high bargaining power. They often compare service providers based on cost, experience, reporting capacity, and visibility. This is difficult for a new organization because it creates pressure to offer both high quality and low price.

Bargaining Power of Suppliers:
For Paridhi Education, suppliers mainly include expert trainers, researchers, assessors, content developers, and field coordinators. Since specialized expertise in early grade mathematics and inclusive pedagogy is not easily replaceable, quality professionals hold moderate bargaining power. Retaining good experts is therefore important.

Threat of Substitutes:
Substitutes include internal trainers, free digital materials, freelance consultants, university experts, and technical teams inside NGOs. This means Paridhi Education cannot succeed by selling general training alone. It must provide highly contextualized, field-tested, and implementation-oriented support.

Competitive Rivalry:
Competition is strong. Established NGOs, consulting firms, independent experts, and politically or institutionally connected organizations all compete for similar assignments. Donor contraction has made this rivalry even more intense. Therefore, specialization and positioning are more important than ever.

3.4 Internal Environment Analysis through VRIO

The VRIO framework shows that Paridhi Education possesses several strategically important resources.

Value:
Its expertise in mathematics education, remedial learning, inclusive pedagogy, teacher training, and local language delivery is highly valuable because these are urgent and visible needs in Nepal.

Rarity:
This combination of technical expertise and local contextual understanding is relatively rare, especially when delivered by a single organization.

Imitability:
It is not easy to imitate because it comes from years of field experience, local engagement, and practical understanding rather than only theoretical knowledge.

Organization:
This is the area where Paridhi Education still needs strengthening. Since it is new, it requires stronger systems for proposal management, branding, documentation, financial planning, and operational scaling.

Thus, the VRIO analysis suggests that the organization has real strategic potential, but it must institutionalize its strengths if it wants to achieve sustainable advantage.

3.6 Present Strategic Difficulties Faced by Organizations like Paridhi Education

At present, organizations like Paridhi Education face a very real survival challenge in Nepal. First, they often struggle to win their initial assignments because they lack institutional track record, even when they have highly experienced professionals. Second, many technically competent organizations remain outside opportunity channels because project information and access often move through informal networks before open competition begins. Third, donor contraction has reduced the number of available assignments and intensified competition. Fourth, compliance requirements such as registration, tax clearance, proposal writing, documentation, financial reporting, and audit readiness create heavy administrative pressure for new organizations. Finally, founder-led institutions often become overstretched because the same individuals must handle technical work, networking, administration, proposal development, and financial management at the same time.

Still, I believe this difficult environment also creates an opening for organizations like Paridhi Education. Local credibility, practical expertise, cost-effectiveness, and measurable impact are becoming more important than before. If the organization can convert these qualities into a clear strategic position, it can build a gradual and sustainable path for growth.

4.0 Chapter Three: Strategy Formulation

4.1 Proposed Strategy

Based on the above analysis, the most appropriate strategic choice for Paridhi Education is a focused differentiation strategy supported by selective partnership and revenue diversification. In simple terms, Paridhi Education should not try to become a broad and general education consultancy in its initial years. Instead, it should deliberately position itself as a specialist organization in mathematics improvement, remedial and bridging education, and inclusive teacher development.

4.2 Justification of the Proposed Strategy

This strategy is suitable for several reasons.

First, from the perspective of vision and mission, the strategy directly aligns with the organization’s commitment to quality, inclusion, and research-based educational support.

Second, from the resource-based view, the strategy builds upon the strongest internal resources of the organization: mathematics expertise, inclusive pedagogy, local language capability, and experience in teacher development.

Third, from the perspective of Porter’s generic strategy, differentiation is more realistic than price competition. Paridhi Education cannot sustainably compete by offering the lowest price, especially against larger or politically connected competitors. It needs to compete by offering better specialization, stronger relevance, and more credible results.

Fourth, using SWOT logic, this strategy helps the organization match its strengths with real opportunities while reducing the impact of weaknesses such as limited capital, newness, and low market visibility.

Finally, from the SAF perspective, the strategy appears suitable to market needs, acceptable to stakeholders seeking quality and accountability, and feasible for a technically strong but relatively small organization.

4.3 Supporting Strategic Directions

Focused differentiation alone may not be sufficient in the current Nepali environment. Therefore, it should be supported by two additional strategic directions.

The first is collaborative strategy. Paridhi Education should build partnerships or consortium relationships with established NGOs, consulting firms, municipalities, universities, and school networks. This will help the organization enter larger assignments without carrying the entire business development burden alone. For example, in a large education project, Paridhi Education can position itself as a specialist technical partner for mathematics, remedial learning, assessment, or inclusive pedagogy.

The second is revenue diversification. Due to shrinking donor support, the organization should avoid dependence on one funding stream. It should gradually develop at least three sources of income:

1. Institutional training and consultancy services
2. School-based service packages
3. Paid academic products such as diagnostic tools, teacher manuals, digital learning resources, and mathematics support packages

4.4 Strategic Position Statement

Based on this analysis, the strategic position of Paridhi Education should be:

“To become a trusted local specialist in mathematics and inclusive learning, grow through professional partnership, and reduce risk through diversified but related revenue streams.”

In my opinion, this strategic direction is realistic, academically sound, and practically suitable for the current Nepali environment.

5.0 Chapter Four: Strategy Implementation and Control

5.1 Strategic Program for Implementation

To implement the proposed strategy, Paridhi Education should launch a one-year strategic initiative titled “Math and Inclusive Learning Excellence Initiative.” The objective of this initiative should be to build institutional credibility, generate practical results, and create a foundation for sustainable business growth. The initiative may begin in selected municipalities, school networks, or development projects where mathematics improvement, teacher support, and remedial learning are visible needs.

5.2 Program and Tactics

The following tactics are recommended for effective implementation:

5.2.1 Development of Flagship Service Packages

Paridhi Education should prepare three to four structured service packages, such as:

1. Foundational Mathematics Improvement Package
2. Bridging and Remedial Learning Support Package
3. Inclusive Classroom and Teacher Development Package
4. Diagnostic Assessment and School Improvement Support Package

These packages will help the organization present itself more professionally and respond quickly to potential clients.

5.2.2 Strategic Targeting of Clients

Instead of approaching every possible client, the organization should focus first on three major segments:

1. Municipalities and local governments
2. Donor- or NGO-supported education projects seeking technical partners
3. Private or community school networks requiring subject-specific improvement

This focused targeting will help use resources more efficiently.

5.2.3 Building Ethical Relationship Capital

Because project access in Nepal is often influenced by networks, Paridhi Education should invest in relationship-building in a professional and ethical manner. This may include regular meetings with municipal officials, participation in educational forums, maintaining contact with education officers and NGOs, and sharing evidence of previous work. In my view, this is a very practical necessity. It is not about political favoritism; it is about becoming visible and trusted in the right circles.

5.2.4 Strengthening Credibility through Evidence

Every assignment should generate clear evidence. For example, if the organization conducts mathematics-focused teacher training, it should document baseline and endline findings, teacher feedback, classroom changes, and signs of student improvement. Gradually, this evidence can serve as both proof of quality and a strong marketing asset.

5.2.5 Reducing Financial Vulnerability

The organization should negotiate advance payments wherever possible, maintain a modest operational reserve, and avoid overdependence on a single client. A practical benchmark may be that no single client should account for more than 40 percent of annual revenue.

An indicative annual budget for this strategy may be structured as follows:

5.4 Procedures for Implementation

Implementation should follow a clear operational cycle:

1. Opportunity identification
2. Client consultation
3. Need assessment
4. Proposal development
5. Agreement finalization
6. Program design
7. Delivery
8. Follow-up support
9. Documentation
10. Reporting

Each assignment should have a designated focal person, approved timeline, client communication record, budget sheet, and reporting template. Standard operating procedures should also be prepared for training management, travel approval, financial authorization, invoice submission, and post-program review.

5.5 Performance Monitoring and Control

Performance monitoring should include both operational and strategic indicators. Suggested indicators include:

1. Number of proposals submitted
2. Proposal success rate
3. Number of institutional clients secured
4. Number of teachers trained
5. Participant satisfaction level
6. Change in teacher competency
7. Evidence of student mathematics improvement
8. Percentage of repeat clients
9. Average payment collection time
10. Revenue share by client type
11. Budget variance
12. Staff or associate utilization rate

Monthly operational review and quarterly strategic review should be mandatory. If expected performance is not achieved, corrective action should be taken promptly. For example, if proposals are not being selected, the organization should review proposal quality, partnership strategy, and client targeting. If cash flow becomes weak, it should reduce non-essential expenditure and increase smaller fee-based services. If donor-funded work continues to decline, it should accelerate its direct market development toward municipalities, school groups, and paid academic services.

5.6 Strategic Recommendations for Real Organizational Benefit

For long-term institutional benefit, Paridhi Education should consider the following practical strategies:

1. Build a visible brand around mathematics and inclusive learning instead of presenting itself too broadly.
2. Prepare two or three strong concept notes and capability statements for quick sharing with partners.
3. Create a database of municipalities, NGOs, schools, and education officers for systematic follow-up.
4. Publish short evidence notes, articles, or learning briefs to strengthen thought leadership.
5. Use small pilot projects to generate testimonials and references.
6. Maintain political neutrality while investing strongly in professional relationship-building.
7. Collaborate with larger consortia instead of competing alone in every major bid.
8. Develop at least one steady non-donor revenue source within one year.
9. Gradually institutionalize systems so the organization does not remain founder-dependent.

In summary, implementation should not focus only on delivering projects. It should also focus on building legitimacy, resilience, visibility, and sustainability.

6.0 Conclusion

This assignment shows that Paridhi Education has strong potential to become a meaningful education organization in Nepal, particularly in the fields of mathematics improvement, remedial education, and inclusive teacher support. The external environment is challenging due to political instability, shrinking donor funding, procurement delays, informal influence networks, and intense competition. At the same time, the social need for quality education support remains very high.

The internal analysis indicates that Paridhi Education possesses valuable technical strengths, especially in mathematics, remedial learning, inclusive pedagogy, and local-language-sensitive delivery. However, these strengths must now be converted into institutional systems, market visibility, financial sustainability, and long-term credibility.

Based on the analysis, the most appropriate path forward is a focused differentiation strategy supported by partnership-building and revenue diversification. In my opinion, this is not only the most suitable academic recommendation for the assignment, but also the most practical direction for the real growth of Paridhi Education. If implemented with discipline, evidence, ethical stakeholder engagement, and strategic patience, Paridhi Education can gradually establish itself as a trusted and sustainable education organization in Nepal.
