1.0 Introduction

The legal environment of business is one of the most important foundations for the establishment, operation, growth, and sustainability of any organization. A business or organization may have a good mission, skilled human resources, and strong social purpose, but without proper legal understanding and compliance, it may face serious operational, financial, and reputational risks. This is even more relevant in Nepal, where organizations have to operate within a complex legal framework shaped by company law, contract law, labor law, intellectual property law, education-related regulation, and changing public policy.

For this assignment, I have selected Paridhi Education as the organization of study. I found this organization suitable because it is newly established and works in an area that is socially important as well as legally sensitive. Paridhi Education aims to improve the quality of education in Nepal with a particular focus on mathematics, teacher development, remedial learning, inclusive education, and educational consultancy. Since it works in the field of education but is registered as a company not distributing profits, it presents an interesting case from the perspective of legal environment for business.

In my understanding, the case of Paridhi Education is not only about legal registration. It raises broader socio-legal and corporate legal questions. How should such an organization structure itself lawfully? What are its obligations as a company not distributing profits? How should it manage contracts, employees, training materials, and stakeholder relationships? What legal difficulties may arise in the context of Nepal’s changing regulatory and political environment? What international legal principles and comparative practices may guide such an organization? These are the major questions addressed in this paper.

The assignment is organized into five chapters. Chapter One provides a brief introduction of the fact of the case. Chapter Two examines the socio-legal and corporate legal problems affecting the organization. Chapter Three discusses relevant legislation and judicial verdicts. Chapter Four highlights international legal provisions and practices. Chapter Five presents conclusions, suggestions, and recommendations. Although this paper is prepared in student style for academic submission, I have tried to keep the analysis practical and useful in the real context of Nepal.

2.0 Chapter One: Brief Introduction of Fact of the Case

2.1 Background of the Organization

Paridhi Education is a newly established education service organization based in Dhumbarahi, Kathmandu. It was established in 2082 B.S. (2025 A.D.) and is registered as a company not distributing profits under the Office of the Company Registrar. The organization was founded by education professionals with experience in teacher training, curriculum development, inclusive pedagogy, remedial education, educational assessment, digital literacy, and mathematics education.

The original motivation behind Paridhi Education was to address the weak state of mathematics learning in Nepal. Later, its vision expanded to cover broader areas such as inclusive education, bridging education, early grade learning, digital learning, curriculum and assessment support, and educational research. The organization seeks to provide research-based, context-sensitive, and socially meaningful educational services in Nepal.

2.2 Why the Case is Legally Important

The case of Paridhi Education is legally important because it stands at the intersection of corporate law, contract law, labor law, education-related regulation, and social accountability. It is not a traditional profit-distributing commercial company, yet it is not merely an informal social initiative either. It is a legally recognized entity that must comply with corporate governance obligations while also working in a socially sensitive sector involving children, teachers, schools, local governments, and development agencies.

From a legal point of view, the organization has to face several questions:

1. Whether its registration and operational model comply with the Companies Act, 2063.
2. Whether its employment relationships comply with the Labour Act, 2074.
3. Whether its contracts with schools, municipalities, NGOs, and projects are valid and enforceable under the Muluki Civil Code, 2074.
4. Whether its training manuals, digital resources, and educational content are properly protected or used according to intellectual property law.
5. Whether its work aligns with Nepal’s constitutional and statutory commitment to education, inclusion, and child rights.

2.3 Core Fact of the Case

The core fact of the case is that Paridhi Education is a newly registered, mission-driven education organization with strong technical capacity but limited institutional history. As a result, it faces a number of practical legal and socio-legal problems. It must build internal systems for lawful governance, formalize contracts and employment arrangements, maintain transparency as a non-profit-distributing company, protect its intellectual outputs, and operate in a sector where public interest and social accountability are high.

At the same time, the organization works in a Nepali context where project opportunities are often influenced by institutional networks, public procurement can be slow, donor funding has become uncertain, and smaller organizations may struggle to establish credibility. Therefore, the legal problem in this case is not only a matter of statutes on paper. It is also a matter of how law, institutions, power, and social responsibility interact in practice.

3.0 Chapter Two: Social Legal or Corporate Legal Problems Scanning

3.1 Meaning of Legal Problem Scanning

Legal problem scanning means identifying and analyzing those internal and external legal issues that may affect the lawful functioning of the organization. In the case of Paridhi Education, such scanning is necessary because the organization is still in its formative stage. If legal issues are understood early, the organization can reduce future disputes, improve compliance, and strengthen its legitimacy.

For this chapter, I have divided the issues into internal legal problems and external socio-legal or corporate legal problems.

3.2 Internal Legal Problems

3.2.1 Corporate Governance and Compliance Problems

As a company not distributing profits, Paridhi Education must maintain a governance structure that reflects its legal identity. One major internal legal problem for new organizations is the tendency to operate informally even after registration. In practice, many small organizations are legally registered but fail to maintain board decisions, minutes, annual compliance, audit documentation, and clear institutional division of responsibility. If Paridhi Education also falls into such a pattern, it may face legal and administrative complications later.

Since the organization is not allowed to distribute profit to members, it must ensure that surplus funds are reinvested for its stated objectives. If the organization informally uses its funds in a manner inconsistent with its objectives or legal status, questions of misuse, breach of fiduciary duty, or loss of trust may arise.

3.2.2 Employment and Human Resource Problems

Another internal legal issue relates to employment. In many emerging organizations in Nepal, technical experts begin work through informal understanding rather than written employment contracts. This creates legal ambiguity regarding salary, working hours, leave, confidentiality, termination, and social security. Since Paridhi Education is likely to engage trainers, consultants, coordinators, and administrative personnel in different forms, the absence of clear employment documentation can become a major legal risk.

The use of interns or short-term trainers may also create legal problems if the organization does not clearly distinguish between consultant arrangements, employment arrangements, and training arrangements. In labor disputes, courts generally look beyond labels and examine the substance of the relationship.

3.2.3 Contractual and Documentation Problems

Paridhi Education is likely to work through agreements with municipalities, schools, NGOs, donor-supported projects, and other stakeholders. If contracts are vague, incomplete, or poorly drafted, disputes may arise regarding deliverables, payment, timelines, intellectual ownership, confidentiality, termination, or liability. A common problem in Nepal is over-reliance on relationship-based or verbal commitments. For a new organization, this is dangerous because the absence of proper documentation weakens legal protection.

3.2.4 Intellectual Property Problems

Paridhi Education may produce lesson plans, training manuals, digital content, assessment tools, research reports, and educational modules. These are valuable intellectual outputs. If the organization does not define ownership rights internally and contractually, disputes may arise about whether the content belongs to the individual author, the organization, or the client funding the work. At the same time, if the organization uses third-party materials without proper permission or attribution, it may face copyright issues.

3.2.5 Child Protection and Ethical Risk

Although Paridhi Education is not necessarily a school itself, it works in the education sector and may directly or indirectly engage with children, teachers, and vulnerable communities. This creates a socio-legal expectation of child safeguarding, non-discrimination, inclusion, and ethical conduct. If the organization does not develop internal policies on child protection, anti-harassment, data handling, and ethical communication, it may expose itself to serious legal and reputational risk.

3.3 External Socio-Legal or Corporate Legal Problems

3.3.1 Regulatory Uncertainty and Multi-Layer Compliance

Paridhi Education operates in a legal environment where multiple laws may apply simultaneously. As a company, it must comply with company law. As an employer, it must comply with labor law. As a provider of educational services, it must be attentive to constitutional and statutory norms relating to education, inclusion, and public accountability. As a creator of educational content, it must also pay attention to copyright and intellectual property law. This multi-layer legal environment can be difficult for a new organization with limited administrative capacity.

3.3.2 Project Access, Public Procurement, and Institutional Trust

A major external problem in Nepal is that access to work is not always determined purely by technical competence. Institutional familiarity, networks, and perceived credibility often affect who gets invited, shortlisted, or trusted for assignments. This is not always illegal, but it creates a socio-legal challenge because formal equality of opportunity may not work fully in practice. For an organization like Paridhi Education, this means that lawful registration alone does not guarantee fair access to work.

3.3.3 Donor Contraction and Legal-Financial Pressure

The shrinking of international donor support, especially after the suspension and cancellation of several USAID-related programs in Nepal in 2025, has created pressure across the development and education sector. This leads to fewer projects, greater competition, more compliance demands, and increased pressure on reporting quality. Smaller organizations may then take risky shortcuts such as informal staffing, under-documentation, or poorly drafted agreements. Therefore, financial pressure may indirectly create legal non-compliance.

3.3.4 Education as a Socially Regulated Field

Education in Nepal is not treated merely as a market commodity. It is tied to constitutional rights, public policy, child rights, equity, and state regulation. Therefore, organizations working in education are subject not only to business expectations but also to social legitimacy expectations. If Paridhi Education is seen as commercially exploiting education, using poor-quality content, or failing to respect inclusion and affordability concerns, it may face public criticism even when it is technically within legal registration limits.

3.3.5 Dispute Resolution Weakness

In Nepal, litigation can be lengthy, expensive, and uncertain. As a result, many small organizations avoid formal legal action even when contractual wrongs occur. This means that an organization like Paridhi Education may suffer loss not because it lacks legal rights, but because it lacks practical enforcement capacity. This is why preventive compliance and contractual dispute-resolution clauses are especially important.

3.4 Summary of Scanning

Based on the above analysis, the central socio-legal and corporate legal problems of Paridhi Education can be summarized as follows:

1. Need for proper governance as a company not distributing profits.
2. Need for formal employment and labor compliance.
3. Need for valid and enforceable contracts.
4. Need for intellectual property protection and compliance.
5. Need for child safeguarding and ethical operational standards.
6. Need to function credibly in a legally regulated but practically uneven environment.

4.0 Chapter Three: Use of Legislative Provisions with Judicial Verdicts

4.1 Principal Enactment: Companies Act, 2063

The most directly relevant enactment for the present topic is the Companies Act, 2063, especially Chapter 19 relating to companies not distributing profits. Since Paridhi Education is registered under this legal form, its legal identity, powers, limitations, and compliance obligations are primarily determined by this Act.

Section 166 allows the incorporation of a company not distributing profits for scientific, academic, social, benevolent, professional, or public utility purposes. This is highly relevant to Paridhi Education because its objectives relate to educational improvement, teacher development, research, and inclusive learning. Thus, its legal form is appropriate to its mission.

Section 167 provides special provisions for companies not distributing profits. The central legal principle is that such a company cannot distribute dividends or profits to its members. Any income or surplus must be used toward the achievement of organizational objectives or institutional growth. This means that Paridhi Education must maintain financial discipline and cannot function like a conventional profit-dividing commercial company.

From a student perspective, the practical implication is clear: the legal form of the organization protects its social purpose, but it also imposes strict responsibility. If members use the organization for private financial gain beyond what lawfully belongs to salary, service fee, or approved expense, the legal character of the organization may be questioned.

4.2 Constitutional Framework

The Constitution of Nepal, 2072 is also highly relevant. Article 31 guarantees the right to education, including free and compulsory basic education and the broader goal of equitable access to education. Although Paridhi Education is not the state, any organization working in the education sector must operate in a manner consistent with this constitutional spirit.

This constitutional background creates two important implications. First, Paridhi Education’s mission aligns with constitutional values because it seeks to strengthen quality and inclusion in education. Second, because education is a constitutionally protected field, the organization carries higher social responsibility than an ordinary business venture.

4.3 Muluki Civil Code, 2074: Contractual Validity

The Muluki Civil Code, 2074, especially Part V relating to contracts, is essential for Paridhi Education’s daily operation. The organization will likely enter into service agreements, training agreements, consultancy arrangements, partnerships, rental arrangements, procurement agreements, and employment-related commitments.

For a contract to be valid, there must generally be offer and acceptance, legal competence of the parties, free consent, lawful object, and possibility of performance. In practical terms, Paridhi Education should not rely on oral commitments. Every significant agreement should be in writing and should clearly mention:

1. scope of work,
2. deliverables,
3. timeline,
4. payment schedule,
5. ownership of materials,
6. confidentiality,
7. termination conditions, and
8. dispute resolution mechanism.

This is especially important in education consultancy and training, where misunderstandings about deliverables and outputs are common.

4.4 Labour Act, 2074

The Labour Act, 2074 applies to organized institutions and is highly relevant to Paridhi Education as an employer. The Act requires proper employment arrangements, working-hour compliance, payment standards, leave management, occupational safety, and social security obligations.

For Paridhi Education, the most important legal lessons under the Labour Act are:

1. employees should be engaged through written appointment or contract,
2. working hours and compensation should be clearly defined,
3. social security obligations should be fulfilled where applicable,
4. disciplinary action and termination should follow legal procedure,
5. interns and trainees should not be used informally to bypass labor obligations.

Since new organizations often rely on informal working relationships, labor compliance is a sensitive but essential area.

4.5 Copyright Act, 2059

The Copyright Act, 2059 is important because Paridhi Education may create valuable educational content such as manuals, modules, worksheets, training slides, reports, digital lessons, and assessment tools. These works can qualify for copyright protection if they are original intellectual creations.

The Act generally protects the economic and moral rights of the creator. Therefore, Paridhi Education should clearly determine through internal policy and contract whether a work prepared by staff, consultant, or partner belongs to the organization, the author, or the client.

At the same time, the organization must also respect the copyright of others. Educational use may allow limited reproduction with proper acknowledgment in certain circumstances, but that does not justify unrestricted copying. Therefore, citation, permission, and proper licensing remain important.

4.6 Arbitration Act, 2055

The Arbitration Act, 2055 is useful for dispute prevention and resolution. Since court litigation in Nepal can be lengthy, Paridhi Education should include arbitration or other dispute-resolution clauses in major service contracts. This is especially useful when working with institutional clients, consultants, or vendors. A written arbitration clause can reduce uncertainty and provide a structured mechanism if disputes arise.

4.7 Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2075

Although Paridhi Education is not a formal school operator in the ordinary sense, this Act is still relevant because it reflects the current legal policy of Nepal regarding equitable and accessible education. The Act operationalizes the constitutional right to education and places obligations particularly on educational institutions regarding inclusion, scholarships, and equitable access.

For Paridhi Education, the legal importance of this Act lies less in direct school-management compliance and more in policy alignment. Since the organization claims to work for inclusive and quality education, its programs, materials, and project design should support rather than undermine the principles of access, inclusion, and educational fairness.

4.8 Judicial Verdicts

4.8.1 Corporate Social Consortium v. Ministry of Finance

This Supreme Court decision is important in understanding the legal status of companies not distributing profits. The Court recognized that such companies, though registered under company law, should not be treated as ordinary profit-distributing business entities when their objectives are genuinely social, academic, or public-oriented. The decision is significant because it reinforces the idea that legal form must be interpreted in light of purpose.

For Paridhi Education, this verdict is useful in two ways. First, it supports the seriousness of its legal identity as a non-profit-distributing company. Second, it shows that courts can recognize the distinct legal character of such organizations when they genuinely function for social and academic objectives.

4.8.2 Supreme Court Directive on Regulation of Private Schools (2012)

In a widely discussed 2012 directive order, the Supreme Court of Nepal directed the government to regulate private schools in the interest of affordable and quality education. The Court addressed issues such as excessive fees, unapproved textbooks, and weak regulatory control. The exact details of implementation remain debated, but the broader principle is very clear: education in Nepal is subject to public interest scrutiny, and private actors in education cannot act without accountability.

For Paridhi Education, this verdict is important even though it is not a conventional private school. It demonstrates that any organization operating in the educational field must be conscious that courts may prioritize affordability, fairness, quality, and public accountability over purely commercial practice.

4.8.3 Legal Lesson from the Verdicts

The judicial trend reflected in these decisions suggests that courts in Nepal are willing to look beyond narrow formalism. Where education, public purpose, or social interest is involved, the judiciary tends to examine substance, fairness, and public impact. This means that Paridhi Education should not only ask whether an action is technically legal, but also whether it is consistent with its public purpose and social responsibility.

5.0 Chapter Four: International Legal Provisions and Practices

5.1 International Human Rights Framework

The right to education is widely recognized in international law. Important provisions include Article 26 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Article 13 of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), and Articles 28 and 29 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC). These instruments recognize education as a basic human right and emphasize accessibility, dignity, equality, and quality.

For an organization like Paridhi Education, these provisions are relevant because they help define the broader normative environment in which educational work should be done. Even when the organization acts as a service provider or consultancy, its work should support inclusion, learning quality, and child-centered principles.

5.2 Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)

The CRPD, especially Article 24, emphasizes inclusive education and equal access for persons with disabilities. This is especially relevant because Paridhi Education identifies inclusive education as one of its thematic areas. Internationally, organizations working in education are increasingly expected to mainstream accessibility, inclusive pedagogy, and non-discrimination rather than treating them as optional add-ons.

5.3 International Labour Principles

International labor standards promoted through the International Labour Organization (ILO) emphasize fair working conditions, dignity of labor, written employment arrangements, occupational safety, social security, and non-discrimination. Even where Nepal’s own Labour Act directly applies, international labor principles remain important as benchmarks of good institutional practice.

For Paridhi Education, the lesson is that ethical employment is not just a legal burden; it is part of organizational credibility. An education organization that teaches quality and inclusion should also reflect fairness and legality in its own internal employment practices.

5.4 Intellectual Property and Educational Content

Internationally, educational institutions increasingly use copyright awareness, licensing, authorship clauses, and digital rights management to protect original educational resources. They also use clear attribution practices when adapting existing materials. This is important for Paridhi Education because its future strength may depend partly on high-quality educational products. If it wants to build a long-term brand, it must treat educational content as a legal and strategic asset.

5.5 Good Corporate Governance and OECD Practices

The OECD principles of corporate governance emphasize transparency, accountability, board responsibility, stakeholder awareness, integrity, and long-term sustainability. Although these principles are mainly discussed in larger corporate contexts, their underlying values are relevant to Paridhi Education as well.

For a company not distributing profits, good governance means:

1. decisions should be documented,
2. finances should be transparent,
3. organizational purpose should guide operations,
4. conflicts of interest should be avoided,
5. stakeholders should be treated with fairness.

5.6 Comparative Practice

In many jurisdictions, organizations working in education and social sectors are expected to maintain:

1. written contracts,
2. safeguarding policies,
3. grievance-handling systems,
4. intellectual property clauses,
5. anti-harassment and non-discrimination standards,
6. periodic audit and governance reporting.

These practices are increasingly becoming standard expectations rather than exceptional requirements. Therefore, Paridhi Education should adopt such practices gradually even if not every aspect is expressly demanded by a single Nepalese statute.

6.0 Chapter Five: Conclusions, Suggestions and Recommendations

6.1 Conclusion

From the above analysis, it can be concluded that the case of Paridhi Education presents a meaningful example of how legal environment affects a newly established organization in Nepal. The organization’s legal status as a company not distributing profits is appropriate to its educational and social mission. However, legal registration alone is not sufficient. To function lawfully and sustainably, the organization must comply with company law, labor law, contract law, and intellectual property law, while also remaining sensitive to broader socio-legal expectations in the education sector.

The main legal issues identified in this paper are related to governance, documentation, employment, contracts, content ownership, safeguarding, and institutional credibility. Judicial verdicts also show that where education and public interest are involved, the courts may give importance to social purpose and fairness, not just formal legality.

6.2 Suggestions

Based on the analysis, I would suggest the following:

1. Paridhi Education should treat the Companies Act, 2063 as its principal legal foundation and ensure that it genuinely functions according to the model of a company not distributing profits.
2. All major decisions of the board or management should be documented through minutes and internal resolutions.
3. The organization should use written contracts for employees, consultants, clients, vendors, and partners.
4. It should prepare standard templates for training agreements, consultancy agreements, and partnership agreements.
5. It should create an internal policy on copyright ownership and use of educational materials.
6. It should prepare at least a basic child safeguarding and ethical conduct policy.
7. It should avoid informal financial practices and maintain transparent audit-ready records.

6.3 Recommendations

For stronger legal sustainability, I recommend that Paridhi Education adopt the following action points:

1. Conduct an annual legal compliance review covering company records, labor compliance, and contracts.
2. Maintain a compliance calendar for audit, renewal, meetings, and reporting requirements.
3. Include arbitration or mediation clauses in important service agreements.
4. Ensure that every staff member or trainer has a clearly defined legal relationship with the organization.
5. Register important intellectual property where appropriate and use proper attribution when adapting existing educational materials.
6. Build the organization’s reputation through legal and ethical professionalism rather than dependence on informal influence.
7. Align programs with constitutional and international principles of educational inclusion, fairness, and quality.

6.4 Final Reflection

In my opinion, the legal environment should not be seen only as a barrier for business. For organizations like Paridhi Education, law can also serve as a framework of protection, trust, and institutional credibility. If the organization builds itself on proper legal compliance from the beginning, it will be in a much stronger position to grow, attract partnerships, and maintain long-term public trust.
